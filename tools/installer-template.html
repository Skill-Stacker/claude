Put StickOS on your USB

Scout, your private voice assistant, runs entirely from the stick. Nothing is installed on this computer.
1
Plug in a USB stick. Any empty stick works. Give it at least 5 GB free, since the AI engine, the model, and the voices land on it the first time it runs.
2
Click the button below. Your browser will ask you to pick the USB stick. That pop-up is the permission. Choose your stick and StickOS writes itself onto it.
3
Open the stick and double-click the Start Button. On Windows that is Start Button.bat; on a Mac it is Start Button.command. That one double-click is the whole setup: the first run downloads about five gigabytes and shows its progress, then Scout opens in your browser. After that it starts in seconds.
Direct-to-USB works in Chrome and Edge. In another browser, use "Download as ZIP", then drag the folder onto your USB stick.
You are viewing this inside a preview. The direct-to-USB picker is blocked in an embedded frame, so open this page from your own browser tab to use it (the ZIP button still works here).

StickOS is on your USB

Now, to start it:
  1. Open the USB stick in your file browser and open the AI-on-a-Stick folder.
  2. On Windows, double-click Start Button.bat. On a Mac, double-click Start Button.command.
  3. The first time, it downloads about five gigabytes (the AI engine, the model, and the voices), then Scout opens in your browser, ready to talk.
Why isn't the whole thing in this download? This installer only writes a few small files, the ones that start everything. Start Button fetches the rest itself the first time it runs. That keeps this page small and quick to load.
On a Mac? If Start Button.command opens in a text editor instead of running, right-click it in Finder and choose Open, once. macOS blocks scripts downloaded from the web until you approve them by hand. Works on Windows and Mac from the same stick.

What Scout actually does

Scout is a voice assistant that lives on the stick. Hold a key, ask a question, hear the answer. It runs a small AI model on the computer in front of you, not in the cloud.

Once it is set up, Scout can also read your Google Calendar and check Gmail, without ever showing you a Google login screen; that part is set up once, with a Google App Password and a private calendar link, both yours to remove at any time.

Before Scout sends an email or creates a calendar event, it reads the exact message or event back to you and waits for you to say yes. It never deletes anything, and it never writes without asking first.

Questions people ask

Does it need the internet to work?
Only for the first run, to download the AI engine, the model, and the voices. After that it works offline, except when it checks Gmail or your calendar.

Is it private?
The AI server only answers on this computer, at 127.0.0.1, so nothing outside this machine can reach it. Every time Scout does reach outside (mail or calendar), it shows up in the "What Scout just did" panel.

Do I need to install anything on my computer?
No. Everything lives on the USB stick. Your computer runs it, but nothing is copied onto your hard drive.

What if I don't have a spare USB stick handy?
Use "Download as ZIP" instead, then copy the unzipped folder onto a stick whenever you are ready.

Everything StickOS writes stays on the USB stick. The AI server answers only on 127.0.0.1.
Prefer to do it by hand? Copy the AI-on-a-Stick folder onto the stick yourself. Same result.
